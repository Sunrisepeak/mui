# omui.ui.canvas

An infinite canvas (module `omui.ui.canvas`, canvas-markdown §1, `OMUI_EXT_canvas`): a scene of nodes in world space — thousands of windows, notes, shapes — seen through a camera. It is the mechanism; selection, dragging, handles, snapping, connectors and the minimap are behaviours canvas-kit registers (`components/canvas/kit`). Contract-first: `contract/canvas.contract.omui.ts` is its interface.

```cpp
import omui.ui.canvas;

auto& wall = omui::world::scene("wall");                      // scenes by name, as models are
wall.kind("note", {.tiers = {{0, omui::world::Tier::Draw::box},     // far: a box
                             {120, omui::world::Tier::Draw::custom}, // nearer: the kind draws it
                             {500, omui::world::Tier::Draw::promote}},// close: a live component
                   .draw = draw_note, .promote = note_view, .role = omui::Role::article});
wall.add({.kind = "note", .x = 120, .y = 80, .width = 240, .height = 160, .key = "todo"});
omui::canvas({.id = "board", .scene = "wall", .zoom = zoom});   // x, y, zoom bind two-way
```

- **The scene** (`world::Scene`) stores nodes by column — world rectangles (x, y in double precision; w, h in float), kinds, keys, fills, corner radii, and the number and text columns a kind reads — so a frame touches only what it reads and a script hands over ten thousand nodes in one call (`push`). A uniform grid indexes them: a viewport query costs what it returns (`tests/canvas_test.cpp` checks it against a scan of 20,000 nodes moving and going; `tests/bench`: panning 100,000 nodes costs what panning 1,000 does).
- **The camera** (`world::Camera`) is the world point at the centre and a zoom, in double precision; drawing is camera-relative, so a wall a billion units out is as crisp as one at the origin. It pans by dragging or a trackpad, zooms about the cursor (the wheel, a pinch of two pointers, ⌃/⌘ with a trackpad), glides after a fling (`world::Inertia`, exponential, it stops), and moves to views on springs (`world::CameraMotion`, logarithmic in zoom) — all functions of the runtime's clock, so a headless run is exact. The canvas keeps the last 30 s of the camera's courses (where it rested, how it moved), so when a time tool takes the page's time back (D-60, `retimed`) the camera is where it was then: before a flight, during it, after. While the page's time is paused, the camera waits and asks for no frames.
- **Tiers** (§3.2): a kind says how a node looks from each size on screen up — an instanced rounded box, its own drawing in screen coordinates, or a live component it is promoted to (the focused node one tier early). A live node is laid out at its world size and shown under the camera's scale, so it never reflows while the camera zooms, and it moves with the camera in the same frame (camera steps run at the start of the frame, before layout). When nodes go live is `promotion`'s: `at_rest` (the default) once the camera has rested 120 ms, so a flight draws textures and never waits on layout; `always` at once, moving or not — what live nodes cost in motion, shown; `never`, textures only (the focused node still goes live).
- **Textures** (§3.2 T1–T2, §3.3): a kind's `content` — the node drawn in its own world units, built on the UI thread — is rasterised on the shared task pool at the mip level the screen's resolution asks for (`world::Textures`), most important first (screen area, nearness to the centre), a few jobs at a time. Until that level is in, the nearest ready one is stretched (a fast zoom shows something at once and sharpens when it stops); before any is, the node is a box. Finished textures enter the image store within a per-frame budget of bytes (`upload_mb`: what a GPU renderer uploads, once per image), and are kept within a memory budget (`cache_mb`), least recently drawn out first. A node's content revision (`Scene::touch`) makes its textures stale; the stale one is drawn until the new one is in. The scene tells its canvases of every change (`Scene::observe`), so they draw again, coalesced to one repaint.
- **Content as lists** (`content: "lists"`, D-188): at the texture tier a node's content is not rasterised at all — its display list is kept, one a content revision, and drawn under the camera's transform every frame (`Textures::listed`). A `content` kind's list is made in the frame that asks for it (never a box, never a frame late); a `view`'s is laid out and painted on the pool as its texture was, and what comes back is the list and the images it draws (which enter the store once). A zoom makes nothing again, stretches nothing and swaps nothing: each shape is where it is at every scale, so the camera's precision is the picture's. It is what a renderer that covers shapes from their outlines on the device wants (`xrender.vector`'s GPU renderer); a CPU renderer wants `textures` (the default). A content that holds what only its own runtime can draw (a lit material, an external texture) keeps its textures. `Canvas::stats().listed` counts them. Lists the pool returns together enter over a few frames, `arrive_glyphs` glyphs a frame at the most and in the order asked for (the first always enters): no one frame prepares all their glyphs for the device.
- **Labels** (`world::Labels`): a kind's labels — a node's name at a size fixed on screen — shaped once a key and kept (shaping is not per frame), shaped again when the theme turns dark or light; `Labels::paint` draws one. The components wall and the markdown wall draw their names with it.
- **Rehearsal**: while the camera moves, a kind's own drawing (`Tier::Draw::custom`) is also made as the camera will ask for it 2, 4, 6, 8 and 12 frames on — the last step repeated — into a list no frame shows. A few nodes at each distance a frame (48, in turn) find what is not made yet: where a drawing made something for the first time (a label shaped, a path built), the rest at that distance are made too, the nearest distance first, within what the paint may take of the frame (2 ms with the frame's own drawing). So what every node asks for at once in one frame was made over the frames before it; the frames drawn are what they were. How many a frame rehearses depends on the machine; what a frame shows does not (`Stats::rehearsed`). A kind's `draw` is therefore called for sizes it is not yet shown at, and must depend on nothing but what its context says.
- **Behaviours** (§1.2–1.3, `world::Behavior`): what a canvas does beyond panning and zooming, named in its `behaviors` (`"select"`, `"grid:40"` — a name and what follows it). A press goes to them in a gesture arena, the highest priority first, until one takes it; that one has the pointer until it is released (a press no one takes pans; a click no one took is offered to them as a tap). They draw under the nodes and over them, add to each node's semantic item what they make possible (its states and actions) and carry those actions out; what they finish is told as the canvas's events (`moved`, `resized`, `connected`, `disconnected`, `deleted`, `selection_changed`). When the canvas lets one go it is told (`unmounted`), so what it put in the runtime goes too. A node whose kind was never registered keeps its kind's name and draws as a box until it is.
- **Semantics** (`OMUI_EXT_canvas`): the canvas states the nodes on screen (and the focused and chosen ones) as its items — `<canvas>/<key>`, the kind's role, a name from its column, bounds — and keeps the rest a query away: `find` searches the whole scene by rectangle, nearness, name, kind and role. The viewport's actions: `pan_to`, `zoom_to`, `fit` (`all`, a key, or a list of keys: all of them), `focus_node`, `camera_preset` (the scene's named views). **The keyboard**: arrows pan, `+`/`-` zoom, `0` fits; Tab and Shift+Tab go from item to item in reading order (rows top to bottom, each left to right; groups and generic nodes skipped), the camera going to each and its focus ring drawn — past the last, or before the first, the page's own order goes on; Enter activates the item focused.
- **Cost** (`tests/bench`, budgets in `budgets.bench.omui.json`): a frame of panning or zooming a wall of 1000 windows, or of 100,000, under 1 ms of CPU (raster aside); measured: about 0.02 ms. After the camera stops at a new level, the textures on screen are sharp again in about 1 ms on the CI machine (`canvas/sharpen`, budget 300 ms).
