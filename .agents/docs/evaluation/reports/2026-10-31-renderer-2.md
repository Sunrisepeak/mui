**Benchmark summary**

Skia’s strongest advantage here is integrated rendering breadth and explicit source/destination colour conversion; Graphite also exposes deferred recordings and resource budgets. [Colour management](https://skia.org/docs/user/color/), [Graphite Recorder](https://skia.googlesource.com/skia/+/refs/heads/main/include/gpu/graphite/Recorder.h).

Vello’s strongest advantage is specialised shared vector infrastructure: Sparse Strips feeds SIMD/multithreaded CPU rendering and GPU rendering, while its compute renderer remains experimental. CPU preprocessing alone is therefore not a valid criticism of openmui. [Vello architecture](https://github.com/linebender/vello/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md).

Blend2D’s strongest advantage is CPU specialisation: analytic rasterisation, JIT pipelines and multithreading, with gradient dithering and broad composition operators. [Implementation overview](https://blend2d.com/about.html), [rendering features](https://blend2d.com/).

Openmui’s distinctive strengths are pixel provenance, explicit deterministic-reference arithmetic and integrated backdrop/style effects. No inspected evidence establishes rendering-quality or speed leadership. Impeller’s emphasis on predictable precompiled pipelines remains a useful reference. [Impeller](https://docs.flutter.dev/perf/impeller).
